# Faculty guide

Faculty can turn an unfamiliar application process into a reachable research opportunity. The highest-value support is usually a specific assessment of research fit, an evidence-rich recommendation and a timely introduction when a real connection exists.

## Advise students early

At the start of the recruitment cycle, ask students about their interests, academic preparation, dates and constraints. Help them distinguish undergraduate internships, graduate direct appointments, doctoral thesis awards and faculty-led collaborations. Refer personal authorization questions to the appropriate university and laboratory offices.

Offer a short meeting with three outputs: a realistic program shortlist, one skill to strengthen and a document/recommendation timeline. Students without prior research can start with course projects, independent study or a campus research assistant role.

## Write a specific recommendation

Confirm that you can meet the deadline and write a strong, honest letter. Review the prompt and student’s [recommender packet](students/documents.md#build-a-recommender-packet). Submit through the official system yourself.

A useful structure is:

1. **Relationship and context:** how long and in what setting you observed the student.
2. **Technical evidence:** a concrete project, method, analysis or result, with the student’s individual role.
3. **Research behavior:** curiosity, response to uncertainty, troubleshooting, reliability and follow-through.
4. **Teamwork and communication:** a directly observed example.
5. **Readiness and fit:** why this opportunity is a reasonable next step, with any comparison grounded in an honest reference group.
6. **Closing assessment:** your support and willingness to answer follow-up questions.

Avoid unsupported rankings, generic superlatives, demographic assumptions or copied claims you cannot verify. Discuss a weakness only with context and relevance. Respect confidentiality and application instructions.

### Illustrative letter scaffold

```text
Dear Selection Committee,

I recommend [student] for [program]. I supervised/taught [student]
in [context] during [period], which allowed me to observe [skills].

In [project], [student] was responsible for [specific contribution].
When [technical difficulty] occurred, they [observed action], leading
to [verified result or learning]. This showed [research quality].

In working with [team/context], they [specific communication or
collaboration example]. Their preparation in [skills] and interest in
[research area] make [program] an appropriate next step.

[Grounded overall assessment and any relevant context.]
Sincerely,
[Faculty name, title, institution, professional contact]
```

The scaffold is a starting point for the recommender, not a ready-made assessment. Replace every placeholder with truthful, observed evidence.

## Introduce students thoughtfully

Ask the student before sharing their materials. Send a short introduction only when you have a relevant connection: the student’s preparation, the research match and a bounded request. Let the lab researcher decide capacity and fit. An introduction does not substitute for the formal application.

## Develop a laboratory collaboration

Start with a one-page concept: shared scientific question, complementary expertise, proposed methods, feasibility, required facilities and a potential student contribution. Contact a relevant lab scientist using an official channel, then discuss scope, mentoring time, funding, access and deliverables.

For [SCGSR](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/scgsr/Eligibility), coordinate with the student and lab collaborator on a defined thesis-related proposal and advisor support. Check restrictions on prior collaboration and the current solicitation before settling on the team.

For [VFP](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/vfp/Eligibility), first verify faculty citizenship/employment and institutional eligibility. Current rules generally exclude R1/R2 institutions under the cited classification, with an HBCU exception. Confirm the current details directly. Develop the proposal with the lab scientist and follow separate student rules if including students.

## Run a faculty application clinic

| Session | Focus | Student leaves with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Opportunity fit | Degree level, research areas, eligibility and dates | Three plausible routes and unresolved questions |
| 2 · Research story | Resume and one project explanation | A revised evidence-based application paragraph |
| 3 · Recommendations | Writer selection and submission process | Complete reference packet and confirmed writers |
| 4 · Interview practice | Technical explanation and mentor questions | Feedback and a targeted practice plan |

## Sustain the pipeline

Coordinate with the Research Internship Advisory Committee. Share approved lab contacts and alumni experiences, track process barriers with consent and update guidance after each cycle. Recognize student outcomes beyond acceptance, including new research skills and stronger future applications.
