# Find the right eligibility route

Check eligibility at two levels: **the program’s funding and academic rules**, then **the specific position and laboratory-access rules**. A lab may host one program limited to citizens/permanent residents and another open to some other noncitizens.

## Understand common eligibility categories

| Category | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| U.S. citizen / permanent resident | The cited route lists these groups | Verify all academic rules and host access; permanent residency is not citizenship. |
| U.S. citizen | The cited route lists citizenship as a requirement | Do not assume a work permit or permanent residency is a substitute. |
| International pathway | The source describes some noncitizen participation or U.S. work-authorized applicants | Confirm the job’s exact citizenship, access and authorization conditions; this does not promise sponsorship. |
| Confirm with program | The overview does not resolve citizenship for the route | Ask the program office before assuming eligibility. |

These categories are research aids, not individual eligibility decisions. Citizens may qualify for international pathways too. Permanent residents should consider both citizen/LPR routes and other routes that meet their circumstances. Use the [shared opportunities spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1XfAbQf3lic7tOpiT_JgilAPFzAPPrtLPZBIZlM1TGBk/edit?usp=sharing) to find programs to investigate.

## Five questions before you apply

1. **Academic level:** Does the program accept your degree level and year? Are recent graduates or terminal master’s students included?
2. **Enrollment and performance:** What enrollment, credits, GPA, age and graduation-date rules apply, and when must you satisfy them?
3. **Citizenship category:** What status does the specific announcement require at application, selection or start?
4. **Authorization and access:** What work/participation authorization and site approvals are needed? Who confirms these?
5. **Participation constraints:** Can you attend all dates? Are there previous-participation limits, advisor approvals or institutional restrictions?

## Routes to investigate

**U.S. citizens and lawful permanent residents:** investigate [SULI](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/suli/Eligibility), [CCI](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/cci/Eligibility), [SCGSR](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/scgsr/Eligibility) and eligible direct positions. SULI is an undergraduate/recent-graduate route; SCGSR requires PhD candidacy and thesis alignment. Their requirements are different.

**Other noncitizens:** begin with specific positions in routes that describe international or work-authorized applicants, including [LANL student programs](https://lanl.jobs/students), [Sandia’s internship FAQ](https://www.sandia.gov/careers/careers/students-and-postdocs/internships-co-ops/institute-programs/faqs/), [LLNL student hiring](https://www.llnl.gov/join-our-team/careers/student-internship-veteran-programs), [INL internships](https://inl.gov/internships/), [SLAC summer internships](https://careers.slac.stanford.edu/slac-summer-internship-program) and [NETL’s program table](https://netl.doe.gov/education/internships/programs). Restrictions differ even within one lab.

**Community college students:** compare CCI with any direct technical roles that explicitly accept your enrollment. Do not use a four-year-only posting as evidence of community-college eligibility.

**Faculty teams:** read the current [VFP eligibility rules](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/vfp/Eligibility). Institutional criteria and faculty/student criteria are separate. Do not assume every university can participate.

## Ask a precise question

```text
I am considering [program/job ID] for [term]. I am a [degree/year]
student at [institution]. The announcement says [quote or describe
the unclear criterion]. Does this opportunity accept applicants in
[relevant category], and which office should confirm any additional
authorization or site-access requirements before the start date?
```

Use an appropriate level of detail in the first inquiry; share sensitive records only through the designated secure process. University international staff and the lab’s authorized office should resolve individual authorization questions.

## Frequent mistakes

- Treating all DOE programs as having the same citizenship rules.
- Assuming permanent residents and students on visas are the same eligibility category.
- Reading “no clearance required” as a guarantee of unrestricted access.
- Assuming a mentor can waive a funding, employment or site-access rule.
- Applying with a future status that the program requires you to hold now.
- Using an old program page as proof of a current opening.

When requirements are unclear or appear inconsistent, cite the exact announcement and ask the official program office. Record its answer and date in your private tracker.
