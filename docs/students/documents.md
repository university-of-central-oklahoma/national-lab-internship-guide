# Documents & recommendations

Build a reusable evidence folder, then tailor the contents for each application. The official announcement determines which documents are required. Do not upload an extra document simply because it appears in this planning list.

## Prepare your application folder

| Item | What to prepare | Quality check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resume or CV | Education, relevant skills, projects, work, research, service and selected achievements | One or two pages is often enough for an undergraduate; a graduate research CV may be longer. Follow program rules. |
| Academic records | Current and prior transcripts required by the program | Legible PDF, your name, institution, relevant terms and grades; check official/unofficial requirement. |
| Research/personal responses | A tailored answer to every prompt | Specific evidence, correct lab names and exact word/character limits. |
| Research-interest brief | One page linking your interests to potential groups | Problem, relevant preparation, host fit and questions; submit only if requested. |
| Portfolio | A small selection of code, reports, posters or designs | Working links and clear individual contributions; share only material you may disclose. |
| Recommendation plan | Required number, agreed writers and due dates | Confirm portal invitations and received status. |
| Availability/enrollment | Academic calendar, anticipated graduation and enrollment evidence if required | Consistent dates across application and resume. |
| Identity/authorization records | Only what the official onboarding or application process requests | Use the designated secure channel; keep these out of outreach packets. |

Use filenames such as `Lastname_Firstname_Resume_2027.pdf`. Keep a private copy of each submitted application and a dated master resume.

## Write a convincing research statement

Start with a real research question. Describe a relevant experience, your specific contribution, a result or limitation, and how it prepares you for the host’s work. Connect to a group’s method or project without pretending to know its unpublished plans.

**Weak:** “Your world-class laboratory is my dream.”

**Stronger:** “In a class project I compared two solvers for a heat-transfer model and found the mesh choice changed runtime substantially. Your group’s work on numerical methods for energy systems makes me interested in learning how you validate those tradeoffs on larger simulations.”

This is an illustrative example, not a claim about a particular lab. Replace it with your own evidence.

## Choose recommendation writers

Choose faculty, research supervisors or other eligible writers who know your work and can describe concrete examples. Follow the program’s restrictions on who may serve. A detailed letter from someone who supervised a project can be more informative than a generic letter from someone with a prominent title.

Ask 4–6 weeks ahead where possible. If the answer is hesitant, give the writer room to decline and find another eligible recommender. The number of letters differs: [SULI instructions](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/suli/How-to-Apply) specify two online recommendations; other programs use different requirements.

## Build a recommender packet

Send one organized message or folder containing:

1. Program name, official URL, selection criteria and exact deadline/timezone.
2. Resume/CV, relevant transcript and draft responses if helpful.
3. A short account of how the writer knows you: course/project, term and specific work.
4. Three factual examples of initiative, technical growth, teamwork or reliability.
5. Your research interests, host fit and intended next academic step.
6. Portal instructions, submission method and your contact information.

Provide evidence and context. The recommender writes and submits their own honest assessment. Follow confidentiality and waiver instructions in the application.

## Track letter submission

Check that the portal invitation reached the correct address. A polite reminder roughly one to two weeks before the deadline is reasonable if a letter remains outstanding. State the deadline and include the submission instructions again. Escalate portal failures to the program office before the deadline.

For WDTS programs, use the required online recommendation process. Do not assume an emailed attachment will count. See the [CCI instructions](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/cci/How-to-Apply/) for its submission rules.

Thank writers after submission and share the outcome later. If selected, tell them what you learned; that closes the mentoring loop.
