# Offers & onboarding

A mentor’s positive conversation is encouraging; the formal appointment documents establish the actual terms. Read them and ask the program office about unclear conditions before committing.

## Review the written offer

| Confirm | Questions to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Research and supervision | Project, mentor, daily supervisor, expected deliverable and review process |
| Dates and work arrangement | Start/end, weekly hours, site, onsite/remote expectations and academic conflicts |
| Funding | Wage or stipend, payment schedule, housing/travel support, reimbursement timing and personal upfront costs |
| Appointment conditions | Access approval, employment or participation documentation, screening and required training |
| Academic coordination | Thesis or course credit, university approval, registration, home funding and advisor agreement |
| Practical needs | Housing, transport, accessibility arrangements, insurance or health requirements when applicable |

Program benefits differ. Do not assume housing, relocation or international travel is covered. Ask for terms in writing and budget for any gap before the first payment.

## Complete official onboarding

Respond promptly to the designated hiring, program and site-access staff. Only submit identity, immigration, banking or similar records through the authorized process. Confirm requirements with the program office when a request is unclear.

International students should coordinate their individual work-authorization process with the university international office and the lab’s designated staff. An offer or a mentor’s invitation does not itself authorize work or access. Start only when the required approvals and official start instructions are in place.

Arrange housing and transportation around the confirmed dates and site. Some laboratories are difficult to reach without a car; ask the current program about options. Understand cancellation terms before paying deposits.

## Before your first day

Ask your mentor for two or three background readings, the expected software or methods, and what you should avoid setting up independently. Confirm arrival location, badge instructions, contact person and the first-week schedule.

For graduate research, agree on how lab work connects to the thesis, how often both advisors will meet, and how data, code, authorship and publication review will be handled.

## During and after the appointment

In the first week, agree on a manageable project scope and regular mentor meetings. Keep a research notebook and weekly progress summary. Ask early about unclear methods or access. Follow site safety, security, data and publication procedures.

Before leaving, complete required reports or presentations, hand off code and documentation, confirm what you can share publicly and return equipment. Thank mentors and recommenders, update your resume accurately and volunteer to share approved lessons with your department’s next cohort.
