    Find & contact a research mentor

    An effective message shows that you understand enough of the research to ask a useful question. It should make it easy for the recipient to see your preparation, availability and intended application route.

    Contact the right person

    Your question Contact first Include
    Am I eligible? Which portal or deadline applies? Program coordinator or student education office Program, academic level and the specific unclear requirement
    Does this job accept my work-authorization category? Recruiter/program office, alongside your university international office Job ID and precise question; use a secure channel for records
    Does my interest fit a research group? Researcher or group lead using their official public contact One relevant paper/project, your contribution and an attainable question
    Can I align this with my degree or thesis? Faculty advisor or graduate program director Proposed dates, research scope and degree obligations
    Who might suggest a research contact? Faculty mentor or Research Internship Advisory Committee member, as available Your research interest and one specific question; introductions depend on existing connections and availability

    Use official staff directories, publication correspondence addresses or program contacts. Do not guess private email addresses. Ask the program office to route your question if no appropriate research contact is public.

    Prepare before you write

    Read the group’s official research page and one or two recent papers or project summaries. Note the research problem, method, one result, and one limitation or question. You do not need to understand every equation; distinguish what you know from what you hope to learn.

    Make a simple fit table: group / current question / your evidence / possible contribution / contact / route. Choose a few strong matches and personalize each message.

    First-contact email

    Subject: [Program or job ID] — interest in [specific research topic]

Dear Dr. [Name],

I am a [year/degree] student in [field] at [institution], preparing
to apply for [program/position] for [term; dates].

I read your [paper/project title] and was interested in [specific
method or finding]. In [course/research/project], I [your concrete
action and result]. That experience makes me interested in [question
or small contribution connected to the group's work].

Are you expecting to mentor students through [route], or is there a
program contact you recommend? If useful, I would welcome a brief
conversation about research fit. My resume is attached [if appropriate].

Thank you for your time,
[Name, degree, institution, professional contact, portfolio if useful]

    Keep the initial message concise, often around 150–200 words. Attach a small resume PDF if appropriate; do not send identity documents, full application bundles or confidential research. Use accurate terms such as “introductory Python experience.”

    Timing and follow-up

    For programs needing a co-developed proposal, begin several months before the deadline. For direct internships, reach out once you have a clear fit and a relevant opening or program route. Required collaborator arrangements should follow the current program’s instructions.

    If there is no response, one brief follow-up after roughly 7–10 business days is a reasonable courtesy. This is suggested etiquette, not a lab rule. After that, try a different appropriate contact or proceed with the formal application if allowed. A researcher’s silence is not an application decision.

    Dear Dr. [Name],
I am following up on my message below about [specific topic/program].
I remain interested in [one sentence of research fit]. If you are not
the right contact, I would appreciate a pointer to the program office.
Thank you for considering my question.

    Outreach does not guarantee selection. Never claim a researcher has agreed to supervise you unless they explicitly have.

    Program-specific contact routes

    • Sandia: use the internship overview and careers links and include the job ID.
    • Oak Ridge: use the RSI page for program questions; approach a research group with a specific project connection.
    • SULI/CCI: use WDTS for application-system questions and the host education office for host-specific research or site-access questions.
    • SCGSR: coordinate a defined thesis project with your graduate advisor and a lab scientist before submitting; see the eligibility requirements.
    • Faculty collaborations: use the faculty guide to prepare a project concept before contacting a prospective collaborator.
