    Your application roadmap

    You do not need a published paper to begin. You need evidence that you can learn, contribute and explain your interest in a particular research problem. Course projects, laboratory classes, campus jobs and independent work can all provide that evidence.

    The timing below is a planning recommendation. Program deadlines, required letters and appointment lengths come from the current official announcement.

    1. Set your constraints · 4–6 months ahead

    Write down your academic level, expected graduation date, available dates, research interests, geographic constraints and funding needs. Privately check citizenship and work-authorization categories against the program rules. Use the eligibility guide before spending time on an ineligible application.

    For graduate students, meet your advisor early to discuss thesis alignment, time away, funding overlap, university registration, intellectual property and publication plans.

    Output: a one-paragraph profile and a realistic availability window.

    2. Build a shortlist · 3–5 months ahead

    Choose approximately six to ten plausible routes, with several especially strong research matches. Include both centrally managed programs and direct laboratory postings when eligible. A route such as SULI may offer multiple hosts within one application; follow its current host-choice rules.

    Record official URLs, job IDs, deadline timezones, reference deadlines and next actions in the tracker. Revisit the live posting before submitting. A program overview is not a live vacancy.

    Output: a shortlist with eligibility confirmed or a clearly assigned question for the program office.

    3. Map research fit · 2–4 months ahead

    Find two or three groups per priority lab using official research pages and recent publications. For each group, write:

    • The research question you want to help investigate.
    • One recent project or paper, including a method or result you understand.
    • A skill you can contribute and a skill you want to learn.
    • A small, feasible summer-scale project direction or question.

    Research fit is more useful than a famous laboratory name. Use the mentor outreach guide to decide whom to contact.

    4. Request recommendations · ideally 4–6 weeks before due date

    Ask people who have directly observed your work whether they can provide a strong, specific recommendation. Explain the program and deadline before entering their names into a portal. Give them an organized reference packet.

    Request required transcripts early. Academic offices may take time to produce official copies. Check whether the portal accepts unofficial records.

    5. Draft and tailor · 3–4 weeks before due date

    Build a clear resume/CV and program-specific responses. Use this structure for your research story: problem → your action → evidence/result → next question → host fit. Follow the prompt, word limit and formatting exactly. Have a faculty mentor or writing-center reader check clarity and factual accuracy.

    If you have no formal research, explain a concrete project, how you handled uncertainty, and what you learned. Do not invent expertise or results.

    6. Submit and verify · aim for 2–3 days early

    Check file readability, program/host names, job IDs, dates, enrollment details and recommendation status. Save submitted responses and confirmation. A saved draft is not a submitted application, and an invitation to a recommender is not a received letter.

    Do not wait for a researcher’s reply to submit a complete application unless the program explicitly requires an established collaborator. SCGSR and faculty proposals require advance collaboration; SULI selection follows its own central process.

    7. Interview and follow up

    Prepare a two-minute introduction, a five-minute project explanation and a few thoughtful questions about the work. Use the interview guide. Update your tracker after every contact, and respect the program’s published decision timeline.

    8. Evaluate the written offer and prepare to arrive

    Confirm project scope, mentor, dates, funding, work location and onboarding conditions. Complete access and employment steps through official channels. Follow the arrival guide.
