    Interviews & follow-up

    The format varies by group: a short mentor call, technical discussion, project presentation or several interviews. Ask the coordinator about length, participants, format and any materials to prepare.

    Prepare three research stories

    Have a two-minute introduction and three examples ready: a technical contribution, a setback and recovery, and a collaboration. Use context → your role → action → result → lesson. Make your individual contribution clear even when discussing team work.

    For a five-minute project explanation, cover the question, why it mattered, your method, the result, limitations and what you would try next. Explain figures and units. Prepare to discuss an unexpected result or a failed approach.

    Practice likely questions

    Question A useful answer includes
    Why this group? A specific research connection and a realistic learning goal
    Walk me through a project. The problem, your work, evidence and limitations
    How did you debug or troubleshoot it? A systematic process and what you checked first
    What do you do when you do not know an answer? Clarifying assumptions, research, asking for help and checking the result
    How do you work with others? A concrete example of communication and responsibility
    What can you contribute this summer? Skills supported by evidence and a willingness to learn
    When are you available? Dates, course obligations and known constraints

    Technical preparation should follow the actual position: coding and data analysis, experimental methods, instrumentation, modeling, writing or other relevant skills. Think aloud when appropriate, clarify assumptions and say when you do not know. Do not disclose restricted work from another project.

    Ask questions that help you evaluate the match

    • What would a successful first month and final deliverable look like?
    • Who provides day-to-day mentoring, and how often does the team meet?
    • What skills should I strengthen before arriving?
    • What training, equipment or computing access will I need?
    • Is the project suitable for a poster, thesis chapter or publication, and what review is required?
    • Which dates, location and onsite expectations apply?
    • Who can answer funding, housing, travel, authorization and onboarding questions?

    Ask the program office about appointment conditions when the mentor is not responsible for them. Graduate students should involve their home advisor in scope and publication discussions.

    After the interview

    Send a short, individualized thank-you within a day or two. Mention one discussion point, restate interest and provide any promised material. Record the contact and decision timeline.

    Subject: Thank you — [position/program]

Dear [Name],
Thank you for discussing [project] with me today. I especially enjoyed
learning about [specific point]. The connection to my experience in
[relevant work] strengthened my interest in the opportunity.

As promised, here is [requested link/material]. Please let me know if
you need anything else. I look forward to hearing about next steps.
Best,
[Name]

    If the stated response date passes, send a brief status inquiry. If no timeline was given, a polite check-in after about two weeks can be reasonable. Follow published program instructions if they differ.

    If you are selected, waitlisted or declined

    Selected: request the written appointment details and follow the offer checklist. Give recommenders a brief update and promptly withdraw other applications once you have committed, following program rules.

    Waitlisted: confirm whether an action is needed, keep records current and continue pursuing other eligible routes.

    Declined: thank the program and ask whether feedback is available. A lack of feedback is common. Review research fit, preparation and timing with a faculty mentor, then plan the next cycle.
